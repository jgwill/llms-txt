# Oscillation vs. Resolving/Advancing Patterns {#patterns}

> The underlying structure produces one of two behavioral patterns. In oscillation, movement toward a result is followed by reversal, and the structure returns to where it started. In the resolving/advancing pattern, structural tension drives consistent movement toward the desired outcome. Naming which pattern is operating is central to structural thinking, and the aim is to understand the structure, not to fix the pattern.

**Misinterpretation risks:** LLMs read oscillation as failure and reach for a fix. Oscillation is a structural outcome, not a moral judgment, and trying to solve a pattern before understanding it biases the observer.

## The Oscillating Pattern {#oscillating}

A person or organization sets out for something, may achieve it for a while, then experiences a reversal and loses what they set out for.

- Movement toward the desired result, then pulled back by the structure, like a rocking chair returning to its starting point.
- The structure is like two rubber bands pulling in opposite directions: whichever way you move, you are eventually pulled back.
- **The pattern is active from the very first step.** It is not "things went well, then went wrong". The structure was built for oscillation from the start.
- The steps can be slow. A twenty-year marriage can be a single oscillating pattern.

**In organizations:** building capacity then downsizing; centralizing then decentralizing; acquiring then divesting; focusing on market, then product, then market again.

When the underlying structure is not changed, change efforts are rejected, as if they never happened. This is structural, not personal.

### Pattern Form {#pattern-form}

1. **Setting out:** an opportunity and a desire that is pursued.
2. **Achievement:** the result is reached and held for a time.
3. **Reversal:** a shift or unexplained event disrupts the progress.
4. **The loss:** the person no longer has what they set out for.

## The Resolving/Advancing Pattern {#advancing}

Movement goes from non-equilibrium to equilibrium by raising current reality to the level of the desired outcome.

- Consistent movement toward the desired outcome.
- Structural tension (desired outcome and current reality, held together) drives the movement.
- Each accomplishment becomes the platform for the next.
- The focus is outcome-oriented, not problem-oriented.

It requires clarity on both the desired outcome and current reality, and an adaptive learning process as current reality changes. See [Structural Tension and the Creative Process](./llms-st-structural-tension.md).

## Identifying a Pattern {#identifying}

A story is workable for patterning when it has:

1. **Specificity of desire:** a clear, specific goal, not a vague ideal like "pursuit of balance".
2. **Completion:** the narrative is fully over. A story still in progress cannot be used.
3. **Granularity:** enough detail to identify individual steps. Look for the missing steps.
4. **Typicality:** the story is typical of the person's life, not an exception.
5. For oscillation work, **not a success story**: the focus is the structure that pulls the person away.

### Generic vs. Specific Steps {#generic-steps}

The observer pictures the specific events to understand the moves, then describes the steps in generic terms so they can be tested against other stories in the person's life. A pattern is validated when it holds across several different events and predicts future events, unless the underlying structure changes.

## Understanding vs. Solving {#understanding}

- The goal is to understand the structure, not to solve the pattern as if it were a problem.
- Avoid the engineering habit of fixing patterns. Trying to solve first biases what you can see.
- The observer's rest position is gathering pictures of reality as presented, without a preconceived formula.
