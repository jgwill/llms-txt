# Field 06 — Robert Fritz's Structural Dynamics

**Sources:** `fritz-1989`, `bodaken-fritz-2006`, `senge-fritz-lineage`

---

## What it is, and what it is not

**Fritz, Robert (1989).** *The Path of Least Resistance: Learning to Become the Creative Force in Your Own Life.* Fawcett Columbine / Ballantine, New York (revised and expanded edition). ISBN 9780449903377.

The primary source for the concepts this portfolio uses throughout:

- **Structural tension** — the generative discrepancy between a clearly articulated desired outcome and an accurately observed current reality
- **Creative orientation vs. reactive/responsive orientation** — organising around what one wants to bring into being, versus around eliminating a problem
- **Advancing vs. oscillating patterns** — the structural claim that problem-elimination tends to produce oscillation because the motivating force diminishes as the problem recedes
- **Structure determines behaviour** — the core thesis; the basic unit is the tension-resolution system

**Bodaken, Bruce & Fritz, Robert (2006).** *The Managerial Moment of Truth: The Essential Step in Helping People Improve Performance.* Free Press (Simon & Schuster), New York. ISBN 9780743288521 (reissue 9781451655353).

MMOT's four steps as published: acknowledge present reality; examine people's thinking about how it got to be that way; create a plan for what needs to change; establish a feedback system to track improvement against that plan.

**Lineage:** In the late 1970s Fritz co-founded Innovation Associates with **Peter Senge**, David Peter Stroh and Charles F. Kiefer. Senge features Fritz's structural approach in *Schools That Learn*, and has publicly credited Fritz as "one of the most original thinkers today on the creative process." Fritz's own background is in music composition; the structural framework grew from that alongside his exposure to Senge's system dynamics.

## Honest characterisation of its academic standing

**This is Western management-and-creativity literature, and it should be described as such.**

Specifically:

- The primary works are **trade books**, published by Fawcett/Ballantine and Free Press — not academic presses, not peer-reviewed.
- No substantial peer-reviewed empirical literature validating structural dynamics as a theory was located in this research pass. Its standing rests on practitioner adoption and on Senge's endorsement within organizational learning.
- Senge's endorsement is a **real intellectual lineage** and worth citing — the Innovation Associates connection is documented and not merely promotional. But endorsement within a practitioner community is not empirical validation, and presenting it as such would be the same over-claiming this packet exists to catch.

None of this diminishes the framework's usefulness. Structural tension charting is a coherent, teachable practice with an established following, and this portfolio's *technical* work on it — `smcraft`, `coaia-narrative`, the STC MCP tooling, the state-machine formulation — is genuinely original and stands entirely on its own merits.

The point is narrower: **the portfolio should not let the Indigenous framing do argumentative work that Fritz's own literature cannot support, and it should not let Fritz's frameworks stand in for Indigenous methodology.**

## The specific risk

The portfolio's central table maps Fritz's constructs onto Indigenous concepts as equivalences:

| Structural Dynamics | "Indigenous Paradigm Equivalent" (as published in `docs/`) |
|---|---|
| Structural Tension Charting | Entering Sacred Space — tension between vision and reality is *mana* |
| MMOT (Truth as a Verb) | Relational Accountability — prevents Wetigo |
| Delayed Resolution | Holding Sacred Tension |
| Creative Orientation | Knowledge to Wisdom Transformation |
| Action Steps | Ritualized Manner — collective focus, not task completion |

Read from the Fritz side, the direction of the claim becomes visible. **MMOT is a performance-management conversation, authored by a management consultant and the CEO of Blue Shield of California, for use by managers with direct reports.** Its purpose is helping people improve measured performance. Equating it with relational accountability in Wilson's sense — a standing obligation to community, land and ancestors — is a substantial interpretive leap that neither Bodaken & Fritz nor Wilson makes.

Similarly, "Action Steps" in Fritz are the discrete moves that resolve structural tension toward a desired outcome. Calling them "Ritualized Manner — collective focus, not task completion" reassigns them a meaning Fritz does not give them and ceremony does not obviously grant them.

See `field-07-braiding-and-critique.md` and GAP-04 for the full assessment of this table.

## Engineering implications

1. **Describe Fritz accurately in `docs/`:** "Robert Fritz's structural dynamics is a body of practitioner work in creativity and organizational learning, developed from the 1980s onward and influential within the organizational-learning community (Senge). It is not a peer-reviewed empirical research programme."

   Stating this *strengthens* the portfolio. It shows the authors know what kind of claim they are making.

2. **Cite Fritz properly** — full bibliographic detail with ISBN, not just internal `llms-*` file references. The repository currently cites itself for Fritz's ideas.

3. **Keep the structural-dynamics work central.** It is the portfolio's strongest and most defensible contribution. It does not need Indigenous framing to be valuable, and it is currently at risk of being discounted along with the claims that surround it.

4. **Separate the two bodies of work in the documentation architecture.** Fritz-derived material (`llms-creative-orientation.txt`, `llms-structural-tension-charts.txt`, `llms-managerial-moment-of-truth.md`, `llms-st-four-questions.md`, `llms-delayed-resolution-principle.md`) is strong, self-contained, and citable. The Indigenous-paradigm material is at an earlier stage. Presenting them as one seamless synthesis currently transfers risk from the weaker claim to the stronger one — in the wrong direction.
