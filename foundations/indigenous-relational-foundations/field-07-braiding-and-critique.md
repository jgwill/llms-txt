# Field 07 — Braiding, Appropriation, and the Synthesis Claim

**Sources:** `kimmerer-2013`, `tuck-yang-2012`, `todd-2016`, `aldred-2000`, `forbes-wetiko`, `broadhead-howard-2021`

> This field assesses the portfolio's central claim. It is the reason the packet exists.

---

## The claim under assessment

From `docs/indigenous-research-paradigm.md`, stated in bold as "The Core Insight":

> **Structural Dynamics IS Ceremonial Technology.**
>
> Robert Fritz's frameworks (Creative Orientation, Structural Tension Charts, MMOT, Delayed Resolution) are not separate from Indigenous paradigm — they function as **ceremonial technology** that bridges systematic Western methodologies with sacred relational Indigenous ways.

Repeated in `docs/relational-science.md` and `docs/ceremonial-technology.md`. Supported by a five-row equivalence table. **Carrying no citation in any of the three documents.**

---

## What the braiding literature actually supports

**Kimmerer, R.W. (2013).** *Braiding Sweetgrass: Indigenous Wisdom, Scientific Knowledge and the Teachings of Plants.* Milkweed Editions. ISBN 9781571313355.

The best-known model of holding Indigenous and scientific knowledge together. But note precisely what makes Kimmerer's braid work:

- She is **Potawatomi**, a citizen of the Citizen Potawatomi Nation — one named nation, not a composite
- She is a **trained botanist** with a doctorate — she genuinely holds both strands
- The braid is **demonstrated through practice**, essay by essay, plant by plant; it is not asserted as a structural equivalence
- She never claims the two knowledge systems are *the same thing*

Kimmerer models a person holding two traditions. She does not license anyone to declare two frameworks identical. **Citing Kimmerer does not itself authorize a braid.**

**Bartlett, Marshall & Marshall (2012)** likewise describe Two-Eyed Seeing as weaving *back and forth* between knowledges — explicitly not merging them. The strengths stay distinguishable. That is the whole design.

**The pattern across the supportive literature:** legitimate braiding **keeps the strands distinct and visible.** It is co-presence, not identity. The word "IS" in the portfolio's claim is doing exactly the work the literature says not to do.

## What the critical literature says

### Tuck & Yang (2012) — the decisive challenge

**Tuck, E. & Yang, K.W. (2012).** "Decolonization is not a metaphor." *Decolonization: Indigeneity, Education & Society* 1(1), 1–40.

The most-cited paper in this field, and directly on point:

> Decolonization brings about the repatriation of Indigenous land and life; it is not a metaphor for other things we want to do to improve our societies and schools.

> easily "grafting" decolonize or decolonization onto any existing colonial theoretic framework could be another form of colonial appropriation

> When metaphor invades decolonization, it kills the very possibility of decolonization; it recenters whiteness, it resettles theory, it extends innocence to the settler, it entertains a settler future.

This portfolio describes its work as "Decolonizing Software Development" and "Decolonizing Scientific Research" (`KINSHIP.md`), and grafts ceremonial and decolonial vocabulary onto Fritz's structural dynamics — an existing Western management framework. Tuck & Yang name this move and call it what it is.

Their concept of **"settler moves to innocence"** — actions that relieve discomfort about complicity without ceding anything material — is the specific charge this portfolio must be able to answer. Declaring a management framework to be ceremony, and thereby becoming decolonial without transferring any authority, land, resources, or decision-making power to any Indigenous community, fits the pattern they describe closely enough that the paper cannot be ignored.

**A PR presenting this work as decolonizing without engaging Tuck & Yang is not defensible.**

### Todd (2016) — citation and accountability

**Todd, Z. (2016).** "An Indigenous Feminist's Take On The Ontological Turn: 'Ontology' Is Just Another Word For Colonialism." *Journal of Historical Sociology* 29(1), 4–22. DOI 10.1111/johs.12124.

Todd (Métis) critiques Euro-Western theory that adopts Indigenous relational concepts — relationality, more-than-human kin, agency of place — while failing to cite, credit, or remain accountable to living Indigenous thinkers and the nations they belong to.

The application here is uncomfortably exact. The portfolio's foundational package is literally named **`ontology-core`**. It encodes "relations as first-class beings" and "more-than-human kin." And across `docs/`, the "Key Sources" sections cite **internal `llms-*` files** — the portfolio citing itself — rather than Wilson, Kovach, Marshall, Absolon, or any living Indigenous scholar.

This is the most straightforwardly fixable finding in the packet, and also the most telling. Proper citation is the minimum form of relational accountability available to a text.

### Aldred (2000) — the commercialization frame

**Aldred, L. (2000).** *American Indian Quarterly* 24(3), 329–352. Non-Native commercialization of Native spirituality marginalizes and disembodies Indigenous histories and contemporary identities. Applies to what an artifact *does* in the world, independent of author intent. Eighteen npm packages distributing ceremonial vocabulary is distribution at scale.

### Forbes — a term the portfolio uses

**Forbes, J.D.** *Columbus and Other Cannibals: The Wétiko Disease of Exploitation, Imperialism and Terrorism* (1978; Seven Stories Press, 2008). Forbes (Powhatan-Renape and Lenape) named wétiko "the sickness of exploitation" and connected the Algonquian concept to European colonialism.

The portfolio uses "Wetigo" in its central table — "MMOT (Truth as a Verb) → Relational Accountability — prevents Wetigo" — with no citation and a spelling matching none of the attested forms (Cree *wétiko*, Ojibwe *windigo*, Powhatan *wintiko*). A concept describing the pathology of extraction is being used, uncited, inside a claim that a management technique constitutes ceremony. Forbes should be cited, and the irony is worth sitting with.

## Assessment

**The claim "Structural Dynamics IS Ceremonial Technology" is not currently defensible as stated.**

The reasoning, plainly:

1. **It asserts identity, not resemblance.** "IS", in bold, is a category claim. Nothing in the literature supports it. The supportive braiding literature (Kimmerer, Bartlett/Marshall/Marshall) explicitly keeps strands distinct.
2. **It carries no citation.** In three documents, across the portfolio's most consequential claim, there is not one supporting reference.
3. **The equivalence table pan-Indigenizes.** Mi'kmaw, Lakota, Innu, Algonquian and Polynesian concepts appear in a single "Indigenous Paradigm Equivalent" column — violating the portfolio's own anti-pattern #5. See field 03.
4. **The Fritz side is mischaracterised.** MMOT is a managerial performance conversation. Calling it a practice that "prevents Wetigo" reassigns it a meaning its authors never claimed.
5. **No knowledge keeper has validated it.** The portfolio's own `LICENSE` records `"knowledge_keepers": ["To be designated by community"]`. By the portfolio's own governance document, no one authorized to affirm this equivalence has affirmed it.
6. **Tuck & Yang describe the move.** Grafting decolonial vocabulary onto an existing Western framework, producing decolonial standing without material transfer, is the specific thing their paper names.

**What the claim actually is:** a genuine, interesting, possibly productive **structural resonance** noticed by a practitioner working in both areas. That is a real observation and worth publishing. It is not an established equivalence, and stating it as one converts a legitimate contribution into an indefensible one.

## Suggested rewording

**Replace:**

> **Structural Dynamics IS Ceremonial Technology.**
>
> Robert Fritz's frameworks … are not separate from Indigenous paradigm — they function as **ceremonial technology** that bridges systematic Western methodologies with sacred relational Indigenous ways.

**With:**

> ### A resonance we are exploring
>
> Working with both Robert Fritz's structural dynamics and Indigenous relational research literature, we notice structural resonances between them: both hold tension rather than collapsing it; both treat honest assessment of current reality as non-negotiable; both locate generative force in a relationship rather than in an individual.
>
> **We are exploring whether these resonances are more than analogy. As of now, they are analogy.** We do not claim that structural dynamics constitutes ceremony, and we are not positioned to make that claim: ceremony is held by specific peoples, and no knowledge keeper has reviewed or affirmed this correspondence. The comparison below is offered as a working hypothesis in early development, not as an established equivalence.
>
> We also hold open the possibility that this correspondence is not available to be made — that a Western management framework and a nation's ceremonial practice may be genuinely incommensurable in ways no amount of careful mapping resolves (see Broadhead & Howard, 2021; Tuck & Yang, 2012).

**And for the table itself:** retitle the second column from "Indigenous Paradigm Equivalent" to **"Resonance we notice (analogy, not equivalence)"**; attribute every borrowed term to its nation; remove *mana* or replace it with a properly attributed and cited concept; cite Forbes for wétiko.

**What this rewording accomplishes:** it costs the portfolio nothing it can currently defend, and it gains the one thing the current version cannot have — credibility with the readers whose judgment matters most. A reviewer encountering "we are exploring whether these resonances are more than analogy; as of now, they are analogy" reads it as intellectual seriousness. The same reviewer encountering "Structural Dynamics IS Ceremonial Technology" reads it as a claim someone made up.

This is the humility the authors explicitly authorized. It is also, straightforwardly, the stronger paper.
