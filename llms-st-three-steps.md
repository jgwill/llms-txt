# The Three-Step Observation Process {#three-steps}

> Structural thinking observes in three steps, always in this order: Start with Nothing, Picture What Is Said, Ask Questions. Skipping a step or reversing the order imports bias, because the questions then come from a model the thinker already held rather than from the picture of what was said.

**Misinterpretation risks:** LLMs tend to treat the three steps as a warm-up before analysis. They are the analysis. An LLM that jumps to categorising ("this is a classic case of…") has skipped Step 1, and everything after it describes the category, not the situation.

## Step 1: Start with Nothing {#start-with-nothing}

Approach the situation without a pre-existing knowledge base, hypothesis, or preconception. Look at reality originally and uniquely, not compared to anything else.

**Discipline:**
- Do not compare what you observe to theories or models you already hold.
- Do not anticipate what is coming.
- Do not sort new information into existing frameworks.
- Keep the aspects of reality that are inconsistent with what you expected. Those are the ones a prior model would drop.

As Newton put it: "Hypotheses have no place in science." An accountant examines the actual numbers, not last year's assumptions about them.

**For LLMs:** suppress pattern-matching against training data, do not import diagnostic frameworks, and do not classify a situation as "similar to" a known case.

## Step 2: Picture What Is Said {#picture-what-is-said}

Translate the words into a visual, dimensional picture: a short movie of what the person describes.

**Why a picture:**
- Visual language carries dimensional, structural and relational thinking that linear language cannot.
- Translating words into a picture raises comprehension.
- A picture holds only what was said, which keeps expectation out of it.

**Discipline:**
- Picture only what the person said, nothing else.
- Note what is present and what is missing. What is missing is information, not a problem.
- Where you cannot picture something clearly, a question is needed.

## Step 3: Ask Questions {#ask-questions}

Once the picture exists, ask questions that deepen it and sort out the underlying structure. The questions are functional and internally focused on what was provided.

**Critical rule:** questions arise only from the picture you built. No fishing expeditions, no questions imported from external sources.

The four question types (Information, Clarification, Implication, Discrepancy) are in [Four Question Types](./llms-st-four-questions.md).

## Applying the Steps in LLM Work {#llm-application}

1. **Start with Nothing:** suppress associations, comparisons and external knowledge.
2. **Picture What Is Said:** build the picture of what is described.
3. **Ask Questions:** only the ones the picture calls for.
4. **Identify the pattern:** oscillating or resolving/advancing ([Patterns](./llms-st-patterns.md)).
5. **Locate the structure:** what is wanted, what reality is, and which concepts are held.

When your own output starts categorising or prescribing, return to Step 1 ([Anti-Patterns and Self-Correction](./llms-st-anti-patterns.md)).
