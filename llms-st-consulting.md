# Structural Consulting Method {#structural-consulting}

> Structural consulting applies structural thinking to the structures that produce a person's or organization's patterns. It moves through three phases: pattern recognition, structural exploration, structural change. Hidden concepts are made conscious so they no longer drive behavior unseen. The aim is fluency in a belief, not its elimination: change the structure and the pattern changes.

**Misinterpretation risks:** LLMs turn consulting into advice, therapy, or belief-replacement ("reframe the negative belief"). Structural consulting does none of these. It observes, questions, and makes structure visible. A belief that is argued away remains in the structure.

## The Three Phases {#phases}

### Phase 1: Pattern Recognition

- See the actual patterns in the client's life.
- Identify recurring behaviors across different domains.
- Understand the predictable outcomes of the current structure.
- Work from workable stories: completed narratives with a specific desire and a clear ending ([Patterns](./llms-st-patterns.md#identifying)).

### Phase 2: Structural Exploration

- Uncover hidden concepts and beliefs.
- Distinguish conceptual risk from actual risk.
- Test assumptions against reality: "Is it true?"
- Picture the client's narrative as if making a movie of it.

### Phase 3: Structural Change

- Make hidden concepts conscious and examine their validity.
- **Become fluent** in a belief instead of eliminating it.
- Take the belief out of the active structural dynamics, so it no longer drives behavior unconsciously.
- A change of structure causes a change in the client's patterns.

## The Ideal-Belief-Reality Conflict {#ibr-conflict}

A recurring structural configuration:

1. An **unwanted belief about self** (for example, "I'm a failure").
2. An **ideal created to contradict the belief** (for example, "successful filmmaker").
3. **Reality** that demands engagement with both.
4. The result is **oscillation or sudden change**: moderate success abruptly ends.
5. The intervention is **fluency in the belief**: conscious awareness, not elimination.

## Key Distinctions {#distinctions}

**Structural consulting vs. therapy.** It is not therapy. It works on structural dynamics, not clinical treatment, and aims at understanding and changing structure, not processing emotions.

**Conceptual risk vs. actual risk.** Conceptual risk exists mainly in the mind, based on past experience. Actual risk is a real, present circumstance. Many patterns are driven by conceptual risk.

**Obligation vs. choice.** Distinguish feeling obligated ("I have to") from genuine desire. Others are free not to like you. The shift is from control strategies to authentic expression.

## Transformation Indicators {#indicators}

**Immediate:** a feeling of lightness; temporary disorientation when old conceptual ground disappears; recognition of patterns not seen before; energy that internal conflict used to consume becomes available.

**Over time:** freedom to be yourself rather than performing strategies; knowing the difference between obligation and choice ("there's nothing I have to do"); a move from avoidance to constructive desire.

## Consultant Skills {#skills}

### Picturing the Story

The consultant pictures the narrative as a movie of the events. Picturing:
- translates subjective detail into structural mechanics;
- shows where steps are missing in the sequence;
- keeps the consultant in the rest position: gathering pictures without a preconceived formula, never arriving "loaded for bear";
- catches the particular flavor of this person's orientation.

### Dogged Questioning

Stay with a question until it receives a direct answer. Clients often bring concepts or side agendas (such as a belief that life must have a "purpose") that defend the existing structure and keep reality out of view. The question types are in [Four Question Types](./llms-st-four-questions.md).

### Negative Space

By observing what a desired result would add to a life, the consultant sees what the current structure does not hold. That is structural information, not a problem to solve.

### Pattern Vocabulary

Terms for describing steps in a macrostructural chart:
- **Deepening commitment:** getting more involved in a committed way.
- **Structural change / "the game changes":** an unexplained event or shift that disrupts the progression.
- **Compromise:** involvement that depends on specific concessions.
- **Disband / end:** the pursuit stops.
